About Dr. Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a modest family, he developed a strong interest in learning and science from an early age. He later studied physics and aerospace engineering, which shaped the direction of his remarkable career.

Kalam became an important figure in India's scientific community through his work with the Indian Space Research Organisation and the Defence Research and Development Organisation. His contributions to India's space and missile programmes earned him widespread recognition and the affectionate title "Missile Man of India."

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. During his presidency, he was admired for his simple personality, accessibility, and strong connection with students. Rather than being distant from young people, he regularly interacted with them and encouraged them to dream, learn, and contribute to the development of the nation.

After completing his presidential term in 2007, Kalam continued teaching, writing, and interacting with students. His books and speeches focused strongly on education, scientific thinking, leadership, and India's future. He remained a source of inspiration for millions of young people until his death in 2015 while delivering a lecture to students.
